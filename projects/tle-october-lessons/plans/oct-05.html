Lesson Plan

Grade: UPK 4
Content Area: W.R.A.P. After-School · Social-Emotional Learning
Date: 10/05/26
Central Focus:

Students will use their five senses to observe fall, then sort leaves by color and size.

Lesson # 3 / 21
Lesson Focus and Goals:
  • Students will go on a nature walk and collect fall finds with their senses.
  • Students will sort their collection by color, then by size, and count each pile.
  • Students will share their favorite find with the group.
Resources and Materials:

Magnifying glasses, collection bags, sorting trays

Learning Objectives:

I can use my eyes, ears, and hands to notice fall. I can sort leaves by color and size.

Early Learning Standard (SEL — Self-Awareness):

Children use their senses to notice their body and the world around them, and describe what they observe.

TimeActivity:
8 min Introduction: Feelings check-in and Listening to My Body — Gabi Garcia (begin).
Teacher: Introduces the day: “Today we are fall scientists! Scientists use their senses.”
Students: Name the five senses with the teacher.
7 min Mini-lesson: How scientists observe.
Teacher: Models: “What do your EYES see? What do your EARS hear? Pick up what your HANDS find interesting.”
Students: Practice: eyes closed, name one thing they hear right now.
15 min Activity: Nature walk + leaf sort.
Teacher: Hands out bags and magnifying glasses; leads the walk.
Students: Collect leaves, sticks, acorns. Back inside: sort the collection — first by color (red, orange, yellow, brown piles), then by size. Count each pile: which has the most?
5 min Game: Find it by feel.
Teacher: Holds a found object behind their back.
Students: Eyes closed: feel the object and guess — “It’s a pinecone! It feels bumpy!”
5 min Closure: “What was the smallest thing anyone found? The biggest?”
Students: Scientist share: each child holds up a favorite find — “I found a ___! It feels ___.”
Assessment:
  • Observation during the sort: can the child sort by color, then by size, and count one pile?
  • Exit check at line-up: show with your hands — was your favorite find big or small?
