Lesson Plan

Grade: UPK 4
Content Area: W.R.A.P. After-School · Social-Emotional Learning
Date: 10/14/26
Central Focus:

Students will identify fall smells and count and compare quantities.

Lesson # 9 / 21
Lesson Focus and Goals:
  • Students will sniff smell jars and name what they smell.
  • Students will count acorns or pom-poms on shadow mats and compare which has more.
  • Students will draw their favorite smell as a picture.
Resources and Materials:

Smell jars (check allergies first!), shadow mats, acorns/pom-poms

Learning Objectives:

I can name fall smells with my nose. I can count and compare which mat has more.

Early Learning Standard (SEL — Self-Awareness):

Children use their senses to notice the world around them and describe what they observe.

TimeActivity:
7 min Introduction: Feelings check-in and Listening to My Body (revisit).
Teacher: Sets up the rule: “Eyes closed! We let our NOSES lead today.”
Students: Practice one big sniff and describe it — “it smells sweet!”
6 min Mini-lesson: How to sniff and describe.
Teacher: Models with one jar: “Sniff… what do you smell? What does it remind you of?”
Students: Eyes closed, guess together; connect the smell to a memory.
14 min Activity: Smells of Fall + Shadow Mats + smell draw.
Teacher: Passes the smell jars (applesauce, cinnamon, pumpkin — allergies checked first!); sets out the mats with acorns and pom-poms.
Students: Sniff each jar and name the smell. Place acorns or pom-poms on the mats: “Which mat has MORE? How do you know?” Count together, compare. Then draw your favorite smell as a picture.
4 min Game: Sniff and guess.
Teacher: Holds up one jar behind their back; children sniff with eyes closed.
Students: Shout the guess — cinnamon! pumpkin! First correct voice wins the round.
4 min Closure: “Which smell was the class favorite?”
Students: Quick vote with hands; count together and announce the winner.
Assessment:
  • Observation during Shadow Mats: can the child count a mat and say which has more, and how they know?
  • Exit check: vote with your hand for the class’s favorite smell.
