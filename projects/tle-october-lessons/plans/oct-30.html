Lesson Plan

Grade: UPK 4
Content Area: W.R.A.P. After-School · Social-Emotional Learning
Date: 10/30/26
Central Focus:

Students will celebrate Halloween with their class — parade, contest, and trick-or-treat — with the feelings theme running all day.

Lesson # 21 / 21
Lesson Focus and Goals:
  • Students will march in the costume parade along the hallway route.
  • Students will use manners at the trick-or-treat stations: “Trick or treat!” · “Thank you!”
  • Students will name the feelings the day brings, using leaf breaths when excitement feels wiggly.
Resources and Materials:

Decorations & party supplies (within the $100 October limit), treat stations, calm-down corner

Learning Objectives:

I can march in our parade. I can say trick-or-treat and thank you.

Early Learning Standard (SEL — Self-Management):

Children recognize big feelings — excited is a FEELING too — and use calming strategies like leaf breaths when excitement feels wiggly.

TimeActivity:
45 min Costume parade: March the hallway route.
Teacher: Lines up the class; leads the route; keeps name-to-face counts at every transition.
Students: March in costume. Wave at the babies — they’re our biggest fans.
20 min Costume contest: Individual + group categories.
Teacher: Runs the contest: every child gets a cheer — “Loudest Roar”, “Silliest Walk” — joyful, no losers.
Students: Show off your costume; cheer for every classmate.
45 min Trick-or-treating: Stations in the multipurpose room.
Teacher: Runs the stations; prompts the practice: “Trick or treat!” · “Thank you!” — manners matter, even on Halloween.
Students: Visit each station; practice the magic words at every stop.
10 min Feelings check: “Excited is a FEELING too — and big excitement can feel wiggly. Need a leaf breath?”
Teacher: Opens the calm-down corner all afternoon.
Students: Point to the feeling of the moment; take leaf breaths if needed.
10 min Closure: “What was your favorite part of today? What feeling was it?”
Students: Each child answers; the class ends the month with one big feelings cheer.
Assessment:
  • Observation at the treat stations: does the child say “Trick or treat!” and “thank you” at each stop?
  • Exit check: what was your favorite part of today — and what feeling was it?
