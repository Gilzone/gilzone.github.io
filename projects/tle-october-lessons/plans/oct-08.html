Lesson Plan

Grade: UPK 4
Content Area: W.R.A.P. After-School · Social-Emotional Learning
Date: 10/08/26
Central Focus:

Students will name basic emotions and practice making predictions — then testing them like scientists.

Lesson # 6 / 21
Lesson Focus and Goals:
  • Students will name five basic emotions: happy, sad, angry, scared, calm.
  • Students will predict whether items will float or sink, and test their predictions.
  • Students will decorate a personal pumpkin and write their name on it.
Resources and Materials:

Emotion cards, prediction chart, sensory table + water, pumpkins, gourds, pinecones, rocks, small pumpkins (one per child), paintbrushes, paint, name cards

Learning Objectives:

I can name my feelings. I can make a prediction and find out if I was right.

Early Learning Standard (SEL — Self-Awareness):

Children name and describe their own emotions and begin to understand that others have feelings too. They use strategies, with adult support, to express feelings in healthy ways.

TimeActivity:
8 min Introduction: Feelings check-in and The Color Monster student retell.
Teacher: Holds up each emotion card. “This is HAPPY. Show me your happy face!”
Students: Make the faces, then retell: “The Color Monster felt all mixed up, so he sorted his feelings by color!”
7 min Mini-lesson: How scientists predict.
Teacher: Shows the prediction chart and models: “I predict the pumpkin will FLOAT because it is big and light.”
Students: For each item (pumpkin, gourd, pinecone, rock) vote with thumbs UP (floats) or DOWN (sinks). Marks predictions on the chart.
15 min Activity: Test it! Then My Pumpkin.
Teacher: Drops each item in the sensory table; marks what really happened.
Students: Cheer the results — scientists change their minds, and that’s great! Then: “Write your name on your pumpkin — it’s YOURS.” Decorate with paintbrushes; set out to dry.
5 min Game: Thumbs-Up Review.
Teacher: Calls out each item — “Pumpkin!”
Students: Hold up thumbs UP or DOWN to say whether it floated. Fastest thumbs first wins the round.
5 min Closure: “What surprised you? What floated that you thought would sink?”
Students: Answer in the wrap-up circle, then line up beside their drying pumpkins.
Assessment:
  • Observation during the game: can each child name at least one emotion and correctly recall one item’s float/sink result?
  • Exit check at line-up: “Did YOUR pumpkin float or sink?” Each child answers before leaving the circle.
