📅Oct 1 Thu
Name and match basic emotions (happy, sad, angry, scared, calm).35 min

📖 Read-aloud: The Color Monster — Anna Llenas

Objective: Name and match basic emotions (happy, sad, angry, scared, calm).

  1. Circle: meet the feelings
    Hold up each emotion card. Say: “This is HAPPY. Show me your happy face!” Go through all five. Let children exaggerate — big feelings are welcome here.
  2. Match game
    Lay cards face-down in pairs. Children flip two; when they match, the whole group names the feeling and makes the face. “What feeling did you find?”
  3. Colorful Jars art
    “The Color Monster felt all mixed up, so he sorted his feelings by color.” Each child paints one jar per feeling: yellow=happy, blue=sad, red=angry, black=scared, green=calm.

🔙 Wrap-up: Which color was YOUR feeling today? Drop a pom-pom in that jar on your way out.

Materials: Emotion cards, clear jars, paint (yellow, blue, black, red, green), pom-poms

💡 Theme launch. Set up the Circle Time Box. Sign of the month: “Dad” (open hand, thumb taps forehead).

📅Oct 2 Fri
Practice calm breathing we can use all month.30 min

📖 Read-aloud: Breathe Like a Bear — Kira Wille

Objective: Practice calm breathing we can use all month.

  1. Read + breathe together
    Read Breathe Like a Bear. Do every breath WITH the children — they copy what they see, not what they're told.
  2. Leaf breathing
    Give each child a leaf breathing card. “Trace the leaf slowly with your finger. Breathe IN while you go up… breathe OUT while you come down.” Repeat 3 times.
  3. Go, Go, Go! circle game
    Children stand in a circle, one child in the middle dances while everyone sings; then they pick the next dancer.

🔙 Wrap-up: When could you use leaf breathing? (Before nap, when angry, when too excited.)

Materials: Leaf breathing cards, music

📅Oct 5 Mon
Use the five senses to observe fall; sort leaves by color and size.40 min

📖 Read-aloud: Listening to My Body — Gabi Garcia

Objective: Use the five senses to observe fall; sort leaves by color and size.

  1. Nature walk
    Bags and magnifying glasses ready. “Today we're fall scientists! What do your EYES see? What do your EARS hear? Pick up what your HANDS find interesting.” Collect leaves, sticks, acorns.
  2. Leaf sort
    Back inside: sort the collection. First by color (red, orange, yellow, brown piles), then by size. Count each pile — which has the most?
  3. Scientist share
    Each child holds up their favorite find: “I found a ___! It feels ___. ”

🔙 Wrap-up: What was the smallest thing anyone found? The biggest?

Materials: Magnifying glasses, collection bags, sorting trays

📅Oct 6 Tue
Tell wants from needs; hear and make rhymes.35 min

📖 Read-aloud: My Magical Choices — Becky Cummings

Objective: Tell wants from needs; hear and make rhymes.

  1. Wants vs. Needs T-chart
    Draw the chart. Hold up picture cards one by one: “It's freezing outside — do we NEED a coat, or just WANT it? Do we NEED ice cream?” Let them argue it out; that's the learning.
  2. What is a rhyme?
    “Rhyming words sound the same at the end — like cat and HAT!” Say pairs; children give thumbs-up if they rhyme.
  3. Clap the Rhyme
    Clap out word pairs (no/go, up/pup/cup). Children clap the rhymes and cross arms for non-rhymes.

🔙 Wrap-up: Tell me one thing you NEED and one thing you WANT.

Materials: Wants/needs picture cards, chart paper, markers

📅Oct 7 Wed
Practice kindness (bucket filling); build AB and AABB patterns.35 min

📖 Read-aloud: Have You Filled a Bucket Today? — Carol McCloud

Objective: Practice kindness (bucket filling); build AB and AABB patterns.

  1. Bucket story
    Read Have You Filled a Bucket Today? “A full bucket means a happy heart. Let's fill buckets today!”
  2. Fill-a-bucket draw
    Children draw or dictate one kind action they did or will do. Tape them around a paper bucket on the wall.
  3. Apple patterns
    Red, green, red, green… “What comes next?” Build AB, then AABB (red, red, green, green). Children build their own strip to take home.

🔙 Wrap-up: Whose bucket did YOU fill today?

Materials: Paper, crayons, red/green apple pattern cards, paper bucket

📅Oct 8 Thu
Predict and test sink-or-float; decorate a personal pumpkin.40 min

📖 Read-aloud: The Color Monster (revisit — student retell)

Objective: Predict and test sink-or-float; decorate a personal pumpkin.

  1. Predict
    Hold up each item (pumpkin, gourd, pinecone, rock). “Thumbs UP if you think it floats, thumbs DOWN if it sinks!” Mark predictions on the chart.
  2. Test
    Drop each in the sensory table. Cheer the results. Mark what really happened — scientists change their minds, and that's great.
  3. My Pumpkin
    “Write your name on your pumpkin — it's YOURS.” Decorate with paintbrushes. Set out to dry.

🔙 Wrap-up: What surprised you? What floated that you thought would sink?

Materials: Sensory table + water, pumpkins, gourds, pinecones, rocks, prediction chart, small pumpkins, paint

📅Oct 9 Fri
Use our bodies to calm down; sing the fall-colors song.30 min

📖 Read-aloud: Breathe Like a Bear (revisit)

Objective: Use our bodies to calm down; sing the fall-colors song.

  1. Self-regulation circuit
    Four stations, one minute each: Stomp it out like Bubbles the Elephant · Stretch tall like TechniCal · Balance like Flexi Flamingo · Roll your shoulders like Ping Panda. Rotate with a drum beat.
  2. Red, Orange, Yellow, Brown
    Sing to Head, Shoulders, Knees and Toes, touching the colors on a leaf poster as you sing.
  3. Freeze dance
    Music plays, children dance; when it stops, freeze in a calm pose — “Freeze like a statue feeling CALM.”

🔙 Wrap-up: Which calm-down move is YOUR favorite? Show me.

Materials: Self-regulation cards, leaf color poster, music

📅Oct 14 Wed
Identify fall smells; count and compare quantities.35 min

📖 Read-aloud: Listening to My Body (revisit)

Objective: Identify fall smells; count and compare quantities.

  1. Smells of Fall
    Eyes closed! Pass smell jars (applesauce, cinnamon, pumpkin). “Sniff… what do you smell? What does it remind you of?”
  2. Shadow Mats
    Place acorns or pom-poms on the mats. “Which mat has MORE? How do you know?” Count together, compare.
  3. Smell draw
    Draw your favorite smell as a picture.

🔙 Wrap-up: Which smell was the class favorite? (Quick vote with hands.)

Materials: Smell jars (check allergies first!), shadow mats, acorns/pom-poms

📅Oct 15 Thu
Express what brings happiness; blend compound words.35 min

📖 Read-aloud: My Magical Choices (revisit)

Objective: Express what brings happiness; blend compound words.

  1. I Am Happy When…
    Start the chart: “I am happy when…” Each child draws their answer and dictates the sentence for you to write.
  2. Compound word blend
    Say the parts slowly — “sun… flower” — children clap them together: “SUNFLOWER!” Try cup+cake, pine+cone, scare+crow, rain+bow.
  3. Act it out
    Pull a compound word from a hat; child acts it, group guesses.

🔙 Wrap-up: Finish this: I am happy when… (everyone answers!)

Materials: Chart paper, markers, word cards

📅Oct 16 Fri
Read body language; move like animals.30 min

📖 Read-aloud: Have You Filled a Bucket Today? (revisit)

Objective: Read body language; move like animals.

  1. Feelings Charades
    Whisper an emotion to one child. They act it WITHOUT words — group guesses. “How could you tell she was happy? Her smile! Her bouncy walk!”
  2. Movement cards
    Wiggle like Om Octopus · Wings up like Two Plus Toucan · Stomp like Bubbles. Teacher holds up cards, children move.
  3. Emotion statues
    Call a feeling — children freeze as that feeling's statue.

🔙 Wrap-up: Show me with your BODY (no words!): excited.

Materials: Feeling word cards, movement cards

📅Oct 20 Tue
Name emotions confidently; sort by color (DOE observation day).35 min

📖 Read-aloud: The Color Monster (polished read — DOE visit day)

Objective: Name emotions confidently; sort by color (DOE observation day).

  1. Polished read: The Color Monster
    Well-rehearsed, slow, big expressions. Pause for children to name each feeling — they know this book now.
  2. Emotion Memory Game
    Small groups, 2 copies of each photo card. “Tell me what you SEE in this picture” — full sentences encouraged.

    Tweezers sort pom-poms by color into the painted emotion jars from Oct 1. Fine motor + feelings review in one.

🔙 Wrap-up: Point to the jar that matches how you feel RIGHT NOW.

Materials: Emotion photo cards (2 copies each), tweezers, pom-poms, painted emotion jars

📅Oct 22 Thu
Observe what's inside a pumpkin; complete fall puzzles.40 min

📖 Read-aloud: Breathe Like a Bear

Objective: Observe what's inside a pumpkin; complete fall puzzles.

  1. Pumpkin parts (teacher-led)
    TEACHER ONLY cuts. Scoop pulp into a sealed bag — children squish it safely. “Seeds! Pulp! Skin! What's the stem for?” Count seeds by tens.
  2. Seed counting
    Glue 10 seeds on a counting strip. “How many strips make 20?”
  3. Fall puzzles
    Cut out the 4-piece pumpkin puzzles, mix, rebuild. Race a friend — then help them finish.

🔙 Wrap-up: What did the inside of the pumpkin feel like? Slimy? Bumpy?

Materials: Pumpkin (teacher cuts beforehand), sandwich bags, seed counting strips, puzzle copies, child-safe scissors, glue

📅Oct 26 Mon
Discover color mixing (red + yellow = orange); explore textures.40 min

📖 Read-aloud: The Color Monster

Objective: Discover color mixing (red + yellow = orange); explore textures.

  1. Pumpkin Paint discovery
    NO orange paint on the table — only red and yellow. “Hmm, pumpkins are ORANGE… what can we do?!” Let them discover the mix. Gasp with them.
  2. Paint your pumpkin
    Paint paper pumpkins with their mixed orange. Write/dictate one word about fall.
  3. Texture Station rotations
    Bubble wrap · playdough · slime · finger paint. “Describe what you feel: squishy? bumpy? smooth?”

🔙 Wrap-up: What color did YOU make today? How?

Materials: Red + yellow paint only, paper pumpkins, texture trays

📅Oct 28 Wed
Paint fall trees; survey the class and count votes.35 min

📖 Read-aloud: Have You Filled a Bucket Today?

Objective: Paint fall trees; survey the class and count votes.

  1. Tree Painting
    Show the fall tree reference. Paint trunks + red/orange/yellow leaves (fingerprints make great leaves!). Dictate or write what is happening in your picture.
  2. Weather Survey
    “Is it getting COLDER or WARMER outside?” Each child places a leaf sticker to vote. Count together — which side has more?

    Picture cards: sort into FALL vs NOT FALL (pumpkin? yes. snowman? not yet!).

🔙 Wrap-up: Which won — colder or warmer? By how many?

Materials: Paint, paper, fall tree reference, sticker leaves, survey chart, season cards

Objective: HALLOWEEN — celebrate, parade, and trick-or-treat (feelings theme all day).

🎵 Songs of the month

Short, repeatable, and built for little voices. Teach one per week; sing daily.

Go, Go, Go!

Circle game song — sing while the middle dancer dances

Go, go, go, around we go, Dance in the middle, nice and slow! Spin around and touch the ground, Then pick a friend to dance around! Go, go, go, around we go, [Name] is dancing, watch them go!

Red, Orange, Yellow, Brown

To the tune of “Head, Shoulders, Knees and Toes”

Red, orange, yellow, brown, Yellow, brown, Red, orange, yellow, brown, Yellow, brown, Autumn leaves are falling down, Red, orange, yellow, brown!

To the tune of “The Farmer in the Dell”

The leaves are falling down, The leaves are falling down, Twirl and swirl and touch the ground, The leaves are falling down! We rake them in a pile, We rake them in a pile, Big and crunchy, watch them fly, We rake them in a pile! We jump in just for fun, We jump in just for fun, Laughing in the autumn sun, We jump in just for fun!

🌟 Classroom extras

After-school visual schedule

Post left-to-right with pictures: 👋 Arrival & check-in → 📖 Read-aloud → 🎨 Activity 1 → ⚽ Activity 2 → 🔙 Wrap-up circle → 👋 Goodbye. Move a clothespin along as you go — children relax when they can see what's next.

Calm-down corner kit

  • Leaf breathing cards (laminated) + the five emotion jars
  • Soft pillow, one stuffed animal, noise-reducing earmuffs
  • Glitter calm-down bottle (shake, watch, breathe)
  • Feelings book basket: The Color Monster, Breathe Like a Bear
  • Rule that makes it work: the corner is never a punishment — it's a tool. “Want some leaf breaths?”

Take-home slips (copy, cut, send)

“Today we learned about FEELINGS! Ask me: What color was your feeling today? — Love, your UPK 4 teacher”

One sentence per week keeps families in the loop with zero extra work.
